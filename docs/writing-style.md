# Dry Trip Writing Style Guide

## Voice
You're a friend who knows London really well and happens to not drink (or drinks very little). You're texting someone a recommendation, not writing a magazine feature.

## The AI slop checklist — NEVER do these

### Sentence patterns to kill
- "This is where you come for..." — nobody talks like this
- "Come for X, stay for Y" — cliche
- "Perfect for..." — sounds like a hotel brochure
- "Whether you're... or..." — the classic AI hedge
- "What it lacks in X it makes up for in Y" — formulaic
- "The [noun] here [verb]s" as an opener — robotic
- "Best for those who..." — clinical
- Starting bullets with "The" repeatedly
- "[Thing] alone justifies a visit" — overselling

### Words and phrases that scream AI
- "Craft" / "crafted" / "curated" / "elevated" / "bespoke"
- "Thoughtful" / "considered" / "intentional"
- "Programme" / "offering" / "selection" when you mean "menu" or "drinks"
- "Groundbreaking" / "innovative" / "cutting-edge"
- "Delivers" / "shines" / "excels"
- "Theatre" / "theater" (about drinks or food)
- "Settle in" / "tuck into" / "treat yourself"
- "NA" as a noun — say "non-alcoholic drinks" or "mocktails" or just "drinks"
- "Abstaining" / "skipping alcohol" — too clinical
- "Centre stage" / "takes a backseat"
- "Proper" (as emphasis) — overused British-AI voice
- "Commitment" / "dedication" (about a bar's drinks) — they're a business, not a charity
- "Atmosphere carries the evening" — nobody says this

### Structural patterns to avoid
- Every bullet being exactly the same length
- Always ending with a qualifier or caveat
- The positive-caveat-redirect sandwich in every bullet
- Em dashes everywhere (—) — one per description max
- Semicolons in casual writing
- Three bullets that all basically say the same thing

## What good sounds like

### Real travel writing patterns
- Short, punchy sentences mixed with longer ones
- Specific details (a drink name, a corner table, a view)
- Opinions stated as facts, not hedged
- Sometimes just one sentence is enough
- Casual contractions (it's, don't, won't, they've)
- Occasional incomplete sentences. Like this.
- First person is fine ("I'd skip the mocktails and just eat")

### Examples of good venue bullets
- "Three mocktails, nothing fancy, but the Turkish food is the real reason to book."
- "They'll make you something if you ask. The actual draw is the bomb-shelter basement and the staff who stay in character all night."
- "Twelve seats at a counter. The zero-proof pairing matches every course. One of the few places in London that treats not drinking as part of the experience, not an afterthought."
- "Big, loud, covered in frescoes. The Virgin Mandarin Mojito is decent. Go for the room."

### The test
Read it out loud. Would you actually say this to a friend? If it sounds like a press release or a TripAdvisor summary, rewrite it.
