# Dry Trip ICP & Personas

Last Update: March 2026

---

## Persona Overview

Dry Trip's audience spans a spectrum of non-drinking motivations. These four personas represent the primary segments—each with distinct emotional drivers, discovery paths, and product expectations. They are ordered by estimated volume and strategic priority.

| Persona | Segment | Core Motivation | Dry Trip Value |
|---------|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Mia** | Sober-Curious Traveller | Curiosity + identity exploration | Discovery + confidence |
| **James** | Long-Term Sober Traveller | Certainty + safety | Trust + planning efficiency |
| **Priya** | Health-Conscious Luxury Traveller | Wellness + premium experience | Curation + aspiration |
| **Sophie** | Group Trip Planner | Inclusivity + ease | Mixed-group planning |

## PERSONA 1: MIA — The Sober-Curious Traveller

| Section | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Bio** | Mia is 29 and has been drinking less for the past year—not because of a problem, but because she feels better without it. She still goes out, still travels, still wants great nightlife. But she's tired of arriving at a "top bar" to find the only non-alcoholic option is a lime and soda. She wants to explore cities the way she used to, just without the hangover. Dry Trip gives her the confidence that every venue on her itinerary will actually deliver. |
| **Personal Details** | Age: 25 - 34, Job: Senior UX Designer at a tech company, Income: £55,000 - £75,000, Education: Bachelor's degree, Location: London, UK (travels 3 - 4 times/year to European cities) |
| **Interests** | Mia follows wellness creators on Instagram and TikTok but doesn't consider herself "wellness." She reads The Cut and Bon Appétit. She's into running, sound baths, natural wine bars (for the vibe, not the wine), and weekend city breaks. She hosts dinner parties with elaborate zero-proof cocktails she finds on YouTube. |

---

### Goals

- Find bars and restaurants where not drinking doesn't mean settling for sparkling water
- Discover cities through their food and nightlife culture without alcohol being the centrepiece
- Plan trips quickly without spending hours cross-referencing reviews for NA options
- Feel like she's making a positive lifestyle choice, not missing out on something
- Build a personal list of go-to venues in cities she visits regularly
- Share recommendations with friends who are also cutting back on drinking
- Maintain her social life and travel habits without compromise

### Emotional Drivers

Mia's decision to drink less is tied to identity, not restriction. She doesn't want to be "the sober one"—she wants to be someone who has great taste and knows the best spots. When she finds a bar with a 15-item zero-proof menu and a bartender who takes it seriously, she feels seen. That's the experience she's chasing. The anxiety of not knowing what she'll find at a venue is the thing that makes her hesitate to book.

### Barriers

- Doesn't know which venues genuinely cater to non-drinkers vs. which ones just have a token mocktail
- Worries that AI trip planners will recommend places that don't actually have good NA options (she's been burned by ChatGPT before)
- Feels awkward asking friends to plan around her drinking preferences—easier to just go along with whatever's booked
- Doesn't want to use a platform that feels "recovery-focused" or preachy—she's not sober, she's just drinking less
- Sceptical of user-generated reviews because they rarely mention the quality of non-alcoholic drinks specifically
- Finds venue websites rarely feature their NA offerings, making research frustrating and time-consuming
- Doesn't want to call ahead to ask about non-alcoholic options—it feels like too much effort for something that should just be available

### Personality

- Curious and open-minded—tries new things regularly
- Socially confident but privately anxious about being judged for not drinking
- Design-conscious—drawn to brands that look and feel premium
- Research-oriented—reads reviews, checks menus, watches vlogs before booking
- Quietly opinionated—has strong preferences but doesn't broadcast them

### Motivators

- Social proof from a trusted, editorial source (not crowdsourced reviews)
- Visual, well-designed platforms that match her taste level
- Specificity—she wants to know the exact NA drinks, not just "they have mocktails"
- Time savings—she'll pay attention to anything that replaces 2 hours of Googling
- The feeling of discovering something before her friends do
- Normalisation—a platform that treats not drinking as unremarkable

### Purchase Path

Mia discovers Dry Trip through Instagram (a friend shares a venue card or plan), a Google search like "best mocktail bars Berlin," or a listicle that links to the directory. She browses the map first—visual exploration hooks her. She tries the AI planner for her next trip, hits the email gate after 2 messages, and signs up because she's already invested in the conversation. She emails herself the plan and bookmarks the site. If the first trip goes well, she becomes a repeat user and recommends it to friends.

### Communication Preferences

- Instagram (Stories, Reels, carousels) and TikTok—visual-first content
- Email newsletters if they're well-designed and not too frequent (bi-weekly max)
- Doesn't engage with podcasts about sobriety—too niche and identity-heavy for her
- Responds to editorial tone: confident, specific, opinionated. Not preachy.
- Prefers short-form content she can screenshot and save

### Messaging

When we speak to Mia, we must treat not drinking as a lifestyle upgrade, not a limitation. Lead with discovery and confidence. Never use clinical or recovery language. Show her the venue, the drink, the vibe—make her want to be there.

**For example:**
"You changed how you drink. Now change how you travel. Dry Trip rates every venue on the one thing no other guide measures—so you stop guessing and start booking."

## PERSONA 2: JAMES — The Long-Term Sober Traveller

| Section | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Bio** | James is 42 and has been sober for 6 years. Travel used to be stressful—not because of logistics, but because he never knew if a hotel bar, restaurant, or evening venue would have anything worth ordering beyond a Diet Coke. He's done the research himself for years: calling ahead, reading menus, asking in recovery forums. Dry Trip replaces all of that with a single source of truth. For James, the Dry Score isn't a nice-to-have—it's the difference between a relaxed trip and an anxious one. |
| **Personal Details** | Age: 38 - 50, Job: Operations Director at a mid-size consultancy, Income: £85,000 - £120,000, Education: MBA, Location: Manchester, UK (travels for work and leisure 5 - 6 times/year, international) |
| **Interests** | James is into fitness (early morning runs, gym), business books, architecture, and food. He's a regular at high-end restaurants and has strong opinions about service. He follows food critics and travel writers on X (Twitter). He's been to most major European cities multiple times and is now exploring Dubai, Melbourne, and the US West Coast. |

---

### Goals

- Plan every trip knowing that every venue will cater to him properly—no surprises, no settling
- Find hotels where the minibar, room service, and restaurant all have thoughtful NA options
- Discover restaurants where the zero-proof pairing menu is as considered as the wine list
- Avoid venues where not drinking makes him feel like a second-class guest
- Share reliable venue recommendations with his sober community and sponsor group
- Travel for work without the social awkwardness of client dinners at alcohol-centric venues
- Have a tool he can trust completely—verified data, not AI-generated guesses

### Emotional Drivers

For James, certainty is everything. After 6 years of sobriety, the temptation isn't the issue—the issue is the indignity of being handed a laminated mocktail menu with three items while everyone else gets a 40-page wine list. He wants to feel like a valued guest, not an afterthought. When a venue has a Dry Score of 5, he knows he'll be treated with the same care as any other diner. That's not just convenience—it's respect.

### Barriers

- Deeply sceptical of AI recommendations because he's seen ChatGPT fabricate entire venue profiles
- Won't use a platform unless he can verify the data himself (needs transparency on methodology)
- Avoids anything that brands sobriety as "trendy" or reduces his experience to a wellness aesthetic
- Has a high quality bar—won't return to a platform that recommends even one bad venue
- Privacy-conscious—doesn't want his email or browsing linked to "sobriety" advertising
- Concerned about data accuracy decaying over time—wants to know when venues were last verified
- Needs the platform to cover the specific cities on his travel schedule, not just hipster hotspots

### Personality

- Methodical and detail-oriented—reads the methodology page before trusting a single score
- Loyal once trust is earned—will recommend Dry Trip to his entire network if it delivers
- Direct communicator—doesn't want fluff, wants facts
- Values expertise and credibility over aesthetics
- Private about his sobriety in professional settings—doesn't want to explain his choices

### Motivators

- Verified, transparent data with clear methodology
- Comprehensive city coverage—he travels widely and needs global reach
- Specific venue details: exact NA drinks, not vague descriptions
- Proof that the platform is maintained and updated (verification dates, correction logs)
- The ability to plan an entire multi-day trip in one place
- Endorsement from people in the sober community he respects

### Purchase Path

James finds Dry Trip through a recommendation in a sober community (Reddit r/stopdrinking, a recovery podcast, or word of mouth). He reads the methodology page first. Then he checks 2 - 3 venues he already knows—if the scores match his experience, he trusts the platform. He uses the AI planner for his next trip and is impressed that it only recommends verified venues. He emails the plan to himself and his wife. He becomes a power user and vocal advocate.

### Communication Preferences

- Email (well-written, infrequent—monthly max)
- X (Twitter) for industry commentary and travel writing
- Would read a long-form blog post or newsletter if it's substantive
- Won't engage with TikTok or Instagram content about sobriety
- Responds to authority and expertise—quote the data, show the proof

### Messaging

When we speak to James, we must lead with trust, transparency, and data. Never trivialise sobriety or treat it as a trend. Show that the platform understands the stakes—this isn't about cocktail aesthetics for him, it's about planning trips without anxiety.

**For example:**
"107 venues. Every one verified. Every Dry Score backed by real research, not algorithms. Because when you've been sober for years, 'it might have mocktails' isn't good enough."

## PERSONA 3: PRIYA — The Health-Conscious Luxury Traveller

| Section | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Bio** | Priya is 36, a partner at a boutique strategy firm, and treats travel as her primary indulgence. She hasn't quit drinking—she's simply decided it doesn't serve her. She wants sharp mornings, clear-headed dinners, and the ability to remember every detail of a Michelin-starred meal. She's not interested in compromise. If a hotel can't stock the minibar with Seedlip and Athletic Brewing, she'll book one that does. Dry Trip is her concierge for this specific, non-negotiable preference. |
| **Personal Details** | Age: 30 - 42, Job: Partner at a strategy consultancy, Income: £130,000+, Education: Master's (LSE), Location: London, UK (travels 6 - 8 times/year, luxury segment) |
| **Interests** | Priya is into Michelin dining, boutique hotels, morning yoga, long-haul trips to culturally rich cities, contemporary art, and the zero-proof cocktail scene. She reads Condé Nast Traveller and Monocle. She follows NA drinks brands and zero-proof bartenders on Instagram. She's a member of Soho House and has opinions about which one has the best NA menu (it's Copenhagen). |

---

### Goals

- Plan luxury trips where the non-alcoholic experience is a feature, not a compromise
- Find hotels where the wellness offering extends to the bar, minibar, and room service
- Discover zero-proof tasting menus and pairing experiences at top restaurants
- Know exactly which venues treat NA drinks as craft, not an afterthought
- Integrate Dry Trip venues into her existing travel planning workflow alongside Michelin and World's 50 Best
- Recommend venues to her network of high-earning, health-conscious friends
- Feel that her choice not to drink elevates her travel, rather than limiting it

### Emotional Drivers

Priya's relationship with not drinking is framed through control and intentionality. She chose this. She's not recovering, she's optimising. When she walks into a venue with a Dry Score of 5 and is handed a zero-proof cocktail that's as complex as anything on the regular menu, she feels validated. She feels like the world is catching up with her. The opposite experience—being ignored, pitied, or given a juice—is insulting to someone who spends £400+ a night on a hotel room.

### Barriers

- Won't use a platform that looks or feels budget, clinical, or recovery-adjacent
- Expects premium design, editorial quality, and brand sophistication—will bounce if it feels amateurish
- Needs coverage in the specific luxury cities she travels to (London, Copenhagen, Dubai, New York, Melbourne)
- Sceptical of any AI tool that might recommend a mid-range chain restaurant alongside a Michelin venue
- Time-poor—needs the planning experience to be fast, frictionless, and high-signal
- Won't provide an email unless she's already seen enough value to justify it
- Expects the platform to evolve and add new cities and venues regularly

### Personality

- Decisive and high-standards—she knows what she wants and won't settle
- Design-literate—judges brands by their typography, photography, and UX
- Influential in her social circle—her recommendations carry weight
- Time-conscious—prefers curated over comprehensive
- Confident in her choices—doesn't need validation, but appreciates recognition

### Motivators

- Premium, editorial brand experience that matches her taste
- High-signal curation—fewer venues, all excellent, not an exhaustive list of everything
- Specificity about what makes a venue's NA programme exceptional (the drink, the bartender, the pairing)
- The ability to plan a trip quickly and trust every recommendation
- Discovering venues and experiences before they become mainstream
- A platform that positions not drinking as aspirational, not therapeutic

### Purchase Path

Priya discovers Dry Trip through a Condé Nast Traveller mention, a friend's Instagram story of a venue card, or a Google search for "best zero-proof cocktails Copenhagen." The design and tone hook her immediately. She browses the directory, is impressed by the specificity of the Dry Scores, and uses the AI planner to build a 4-day Copenhagen itinerary. She emails the plan to herself and her PA. She shares the platform with 3 friends over dinner that week. If Dry Trip adds cities she cares about (Tokyo, Barcelona, Paris), she becomes a long-term evangelist.

### Communication Preferences

- Instagram (curated, editorial content—venue photography, drink close-ups, city guides)
- Email newsletter if it's beautifully designed and monthly
- Would click through from a Condé Nast Traveller, Monocle, or Wallpaper* feature
- Won't engage with anything that looks mass-market, discount-driven, or templated
- Responds to aspiration and exclusivity—"the venues insiders know about"

### Messaging

When we speak to Priya, we must lead with aspiration and specificity. She doesn't need convincing that not drinking is valid—she needs us to match her standards. Show her the drink, the venue, the score. Make it feel like a luxury concierge service, not a sobriety tool.

**For example:**
"Clear-headed luxury travel. Dry Trip scores the world's best hotels, restaurants, and bars on the one thing no other guide measures—so you never settle for less than world-class."

## PERSONA 4: SOPHIE — The Group Trip Planner

| Section | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Bio** | Sophie is 31 and the self-appointed organiser of every group trip, hen do, and birthday weekend in her friend group. She doesn't drink much herself, but the real problem is that half her friends do and half don't—and she's sick of planning evenings where someone ends up bored. She needs venues where the cocktail menu and the zero-proof menu are both excellent. Dry Trip solves the one planning problem Google can't: finding places that genuinely work for everyone. |
| **Personal Details** | Age: 27 - 38, Job: Marketing Manager at a DTC brand, Income: £45,000 - £60,000, Education: Bachelor's, Location: Bristol, UK (plans 2 - 3 group trips/year, mainly European cities) |
| **Interests** | Sophie is a planner by nature—she uses Notion for everything, has a shared Google Sheet for her friend group's trip budget, and screenshots restaurant menus before booking. She's into food trends, group fitness classes, weekend markets, and cosy pub gardens (for the atmosphere, not the pints). She watches travel vlogs on YouTube and follows "things to do in [city]" accounts on Instagram. |

---

### Goals

- Find venues that work for both drinkers and non-drinkers without anyone feeling like they're compromising
- Plan group trips efficiently without spending days researching whether each venue has good NA options
- Avoid the awkwardness of suggesting a "sober bar" to friends who do drink—find places where it's just part of the offering
- Build complete itineraries she can share with the group in a single link or email
- Discover venues in cities she hasn't been to before, with enough detail to book confidently
- Feel like the hero of the friend group for finding amazing spots nobody else knew about
- Reduce the mental load of being the one who always organises everything

### Emotional Drivers

Sophie's motivation is social harmony. She wants everyone to have a great time, and she takes it personally when someone doesn't. The worst feeling is booking a "top bar" from a Google listicle and discovering that the non-drinking friends are stuck with tap water while everyone else has cocktails. Dry Trip removes that risk. When she finds a venue with a Dry Score of 4+ that also has great regular cocktails, she's found gold. She wants to share the plan and have everyone say "this looks amazing."

### Barriers

- Doesn't want to make the trip "about" not drinking—needs the platform to feel inclusive, not niche
- Price-conscious for group trips—needs a mix of price ranges, not just luxury
- Needs to share plans easily with a non-technical friend group (email, WhatsApp link, not an app download)
- Won't commit time to a platform unless it covers the specific city she's planning for right now
- Wary of AI tools after seeing ChatGPT suggest a "rooftop bar" that turned out to be a hotel lobby
- Needs social proof that the venues are actually good, not just "good for sober people"
- Time-pressured—usually planning in stolen moments between work, not in dedicated research sessions

### Personality

- Organised and proactive—the one who starts the WhatsApp group and pins the itinerary
- People-pleaser with high empathy—attuned to everyone's preferences and dietary needs
- Practical and resourceful—uses tools, templates, and lists to manage complexity
- Sociable and extroverted—energised by group experiences
- Trend-aware—wants to try the new thing before it's on everyone's radar

### Motivators

- Speed—anything that replaces 3 hours of Googling with a 5-minute plan
- Shareability—the ability to send a complete plan to 8 friends in one message
- Inclusivity—venues scored for both the drinking and non-drinking experience
- Specificity—"this bar has 12 zero-proof cocktails AND an award-winning wine list" is exactly what she needs
- Social capital—recommending a venue nobody else has heard of
- Reduced cognitive load—the less she has to think about the NA angle, the better

### Purchase Path

Sophie discovers Dry Trip through a Google search like "best restaurants for groups London non-drinkers," a friend's recommendation, or a TikTok/Instagram Reel about alcohol-free nightlife. She goes straight to the AI planner and describes her trip: "4 friends, 3-day weekend in Berlin, two of us don't drink." The AI builds an itinerary that balances everyone's preferences. She hits the email gate, signs up immediately because she wants to send the plan to the WhatsApp group. She copies the plan, shares it, and gets praised for her planning skills. She uses Dry Trip for every group trip going forward.

### Communication Preferences

- Instagram and TikTok (short, visual, shareable content—"3 bars in London for mixed groups")
- WhatsApp-friendly content she can forward to friends
- Email if it includes actionable trip ideas she can use immediately
- YouTube travel vlogs if they feature specific venues and are under 10 minutes
- Responds to practical, inclusive messaging—"works for everyone" is her trigger phrase

### Messaging

When we speak to Sophie, we must lead with ease and inclusivity. She's not primarily a non-drinker herself—she's a planner who needs the group to work. Don't make it about sobriety. Make it about solving her planning problem and making her the hero.

**For example:**
"Planning a group trip where half your friends drink and half don't? Dry Trip finds the venues that work for everyone—so you stop stressing and start booking."
