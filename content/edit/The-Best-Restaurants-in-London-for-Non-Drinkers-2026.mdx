Most restaurants still treat non-alcoholic drinks as filler. You get three mocktails at the bottom of the cocktail list: one too sweet, one too juicy, and a virgin mojito that's basically lime soda. The food might be great, but the drink side of the experience is flat.

London is ahead of most cities on this. A growing number of its best restaurants have put real work into their AF programmes: NA spirit-based cocktails, zero-proof wine pairings built by sommeliers, and staff who can actually talk about what they're pouring.

Here's where to find them.

## Fine Dining with NA Pairings

### [Evelyn's Table](/venues/evelyns-table-london)

**Chinatown · Dry Score: 4/5**

Counter dining for 12 guests in a basement kitchen beneath The Blue Posts pub. They offer a No & Low ABV pairing alongside the tasting menu, designed course by course. The by-the-glass NA options show original flavour work — quince with sobacha and peppercorn, gooseberry with jalapeño — not commercial spirit builds. At this scale (12 covers), the investment in an AF pairing says something about where they think the industry is going.

**Order:** The No & Low ABV pairing with the tasting menu. You can also do a Half & Half, mixing wine and NA pairings across courses.

**Best for:** A special meal where you want the full multi-course pairing without alcohol.

### [Restaurant Gordon Ramsay](/venues/restaurant-gordon-ramsay-london)

**Chelsea · Dry Score: 4/5**

Three Michelin stars. Two NA pairing tiers sit alongside the wine list as an actual alternative, not a consolation. Eight by-the-glass NA options including Copenhagen Sparkling Tea, Saicho, and Jukes. At this level of dining, that's a signal.

**Order:** The sommelier-composed NA pairing with the tasting menu.

**Best for:** London's most prestigious restaurant with AF drinks given the same weight as wine.

### [The Clove Club](/venues/the-clove-club-london)

**Shoreditch · Dry Score: 4/5**

Two Michelin stars. Modern British tasting menu in Shoreditch Town Hall. Their Ambient Tea Pairing — cold-infused teas served at room temperature in wine glasses — is designed as a complex, grown-up non-alcoholic experience. Forbes featured it as best-in-class. Mixed tables can combine wine and tea pairings without anyone feeling like an afterthought.

**Order:** The Ambient Tea Pairing.

**Best for:** Michelin dining in a less buttoned-up setting with one of London's most distinctive AF pairing concepts.

### [Akoko](/venues/akoko-london)

**Fitzrovia · Dry Score: 4/5**

Michelin-starred West African cuisine with NA pairings drawn from across the African continent. The food here uses flavours and ingredients you won't find anywhere else in London, and the AF drinks extend that by pulling from West African drink traditions rather than defaulting to the usual Seedlip-and-tonic formula.

**Order:** The £60 soft pairing — fat-washed coconut water, tamarind with penja pepper, seasonal spice-led serves.

**Best for:** Something genuinely different. Food and drinks you can't get elsewhere.

### [Frog by Adam Handling](/venues/frog-by-adam-handling-london)

**Covent Garden · Dry Score: 4/5**

Adam Handling's Michelin-starred flagship. The AF tasting pairing runs as a structural peer to the wine pairings — not a concession menu, an actual parallel track. Eleven curated NA by-the-glass options organised by wine category (sparkling, white, rosé, red, sweet), featuring French Bloom, Jukes, NON, and Saicho. Sommelier-led, with the same service as the wine flight.

**Order:** The AF tasting pairing with the seasonal menu.

**Best for:** A Michelin tasting menu where the AF pairing is taken as seriously as the wine.

## Restaurants with Standout AF Programmes

### [Dishoom](/venues/dishoom-covent-garden)

**Multiple locations · Dry Score: 5/5**

Dishoom's Teetotal Tipples programme runs over twenty dedicated serves: house-made no-alcohol gin negronis, sharbats, Pani Puri Poptails, lion's mane and ashwagandha functional shots, Crossip spirit cocktails, and Baba's single-estate coffee. The menu has its own design treatment and runs identically across every location.

**Order:** The Pani Puri Poptails or the house-made no-alcohol gin negroni.

**Best for:** A full evening of NA drinks without repetition, in a restaurant where the AF menu is part of the identity.

**Locations:** [King's Cross](/venues/dishoom-kings-cross), [Shoreditch](/venues/dishoom-shoreditch), [Covent Garden](/venues/dishoom-covent-garden), Kensington, Carnaby.

### [Chutney Mary](/venues/chutney-mary-london)

**St James's · Dry Score: 4/5**

Indian fine dining since 1990. Six house-made NA cocktails built from Indian botanicals — curry leaf, Bengali lime, saffron — with no commercial NA spirit brands in sight. The AF drinks share the same botanical DNA as the Pukka Bar's cocktail programme. Wild Idol sparkling and Lucky Saint lager cover the wine and beer slots.

**Order:** The Jal Jeera Pani or the saffron and cardamom mojito.

**Best for:** Special-occasion Indian dining where the AF drinks are built from the same ingredients as the food.

### [Cellar at Kindred](/venues/cellar-at-kindred-london)

**Hammersmith · Dry Score: 3/5**

Underground restaurant beneath Kindred. Vegetable-forward cooking with NA cocktails, AF wines, and 0.0% beer — broad coverage across every drinks category in a beautifully designed 35-page drinks booklet. Values-driven, championing independent London suppliers.

**Order:** The Lively Spritz with guayusa and apple kombucha.

**Best for:** Plant-forward food with AF drinks that actually match.

## Hotels with AF-Friendly Dining

### The Savoy — American Bar

**Covent Garden · Dry Score: 4/5**

The American Bar (ranked in the world's 50 best, open since 1893) has a dedicated Spirit Free section on its "Liquid Moments" menu. NA cocktails built with Seedlip, Æcorn Aperitifs, and house ingredients, plus AF sparkling wine and AF beer. Live jazz piano. The Beaufort Bar has its own AF list.

**Order:** A Spirit Free cocktail from the Liquid Moments menu.

**Best for:** Dinner at the hotel followed by drinks in one of the world's most famous bars. Nothing about the evening changes because you're not drinking.

### [Claridge's](/venues/claridges-bar-mayfair)

**Mayfair · Dry Score: 3/5**

Art Deco hotel with multiple bars and restaurants, each with their own NA offering. Claridge's Bar has five named NA cocktails (£15 each) using Seedlip, Tanqueray 0.0%, and Clean Co, plus Wild Idol sparkling by the glass. The Fumoir and Claridge's Restaurant have their own dedicated sections too. Well-presented and clearly labelled "Non-Alcoholic" rather than "mocktails." Afternoon tea here is naturally AF-friendly and worth doing on its own.

**Order:** A Non-Alcoholic cocktail at Claridge's Bar, or afternoon tea in The Foyer.

**Best for:** The full Claridge's experience — Art Deco interiors, impeccable service, and AF options across every venue in the building.

### [Ham Yard Hotel](/venues/ham-yard-hotel-london)

**Soho · Dry Score: 3/5**

Firmdale's design-led Soho hotel. The bar serves four named NA cocktails (£12 each) built with Everleaf, Seedlip, and Martini Vibrante, with house touches like homemade tonic water and apple and lemongrass cordial. The interiors — Kit Kemp's signature colour and pattern — are worth the visit on their own.

**Order:** The Ham Yard Abstinence Tonic, made with homemade tonic water.

**Best for:** A stylish Soho hotel bar where you can settle in for the evening with well-made AF cocktails and one of London's best-designed rooms.

## What to Look For

Not every restaurant that lists mocktails is actually good for non-drinkers. A few things that separate the real programmes from the performative ones:

* A separate NA section on the menu. If the AF drinks are buried at the bottom of the cocktail list or grouped with soft drinks, they haven't been prioritised.
* NA spirits, not just juice. Drinks built around Seedlip, Everleaf, Lyre's, Three Spirit or similar NA brands have more depth than juice-and-soda builds.
* Staff who can describe the AF options without looking confused. This is the simplest test. If the server doesn't know what's on the NA menu, neither does the kitchen.
* Reasonable pricing. NA cocktails should cost less than alcoholic ones (the ingredients cost less), but not so cheap they signal "this isn't a real drink." £8 to £15 in London is fair.
* No commentary. The best places are the ones where ordering AF is treated as normal. No raised eyebrows, no "are you sure?", no making you feel like you're opting out.

## Quick Picks

**Splurge:** [Evelyn's Table](/venues/evelyns-table-london) or [Restaurant Gordon Ramsay](/venues/restaurant-gordon-ramsay-london). Full NA pairings built course by course.

**Everyday:** [Dishoom](/venues/dishoom-covent-garden). Twenty-plus AF drinks, house-made ingredients, no fuss.

**Something different:** [Akoko](/venues/akoko-london). West African cuisine with NA pairings you won't find anywhere else.

**Full evening:** [Frog by Adam Handling](/venues/frog-by-adam-handling-london). Michelin tasting menu with an AF pairing that runs as a peer to the wine.

**Hotel evening:** The Savoy. Dinner, then the American Bar with live jazz piano.
